After years of education, People are still messing up with grammatical errors. Even the most educated or native speaking people who know more about the English language<\/a> do make some grammar mistakes. Common mistakes like words, spelling, and punctuation, etc. People are not even aware that they are making errors and it is common to everyone.<\/p>\r\n

Here we\u2019ve assembled the 10 most common grammar mistakes made by everyone and some steps to avoid it.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

    \r\n \t
  1. Your\/You\u2019re<\/strong><\/li>\r\n<\/ol>\r\n

    Your- is the possessive adjective. It is used to describe that \u201csomething belongs to you\u201d. It is followed by nouns and gerunds.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

      \r\n \t
    • Where is your pot?<\/li>\r\n \t
    • That is your book.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

      You\u2019re- is the contraction of \u201cYOU ARE\u201d as YOU\u2019RE.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

        \r\n \t
      • What you\u2019re doing?<\/li>\r\n \t
      • You\u2019re Stupid.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n
          \r\n \t
        1. Complement\/Compliments<\/strong><\/li>\r\n<\/ol>\r\n

          Complement: is something that adds to or supplements something else. It can act as either a noun or a verb.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

            \r\n \t
          • \u00a0Red wine is a good complement to a streak.<\/li>\r\n \t
          • Rice makes an excellent complement to a curry dish.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

            Compliment: It is something nice someone says about you.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

              \r\n \t
            • She receives compliments on her good sense of fashion.<\/li>\r\n \t
            • She gave me nice compliments<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n
                \r\n \t
              1. Affect\/Effect<\/strong><\/li>\r\n<\/ol>\r\n

                Affect- is used as a verb. To influence or cause, that means it is acting on something or someone.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                  \r\n \t
                • Alcohol affects the brain and heart.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                  Effect- It is usually used as a noun. It shows the result of something that has occurred.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                    \r\n \t
                  • This medicine has no side effects<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n
                      \r\n \t
                    1. Comma Fault<\/strong><\/li>\r\n<\/ol>\r\n

                      A comma is used to connect or join the two independent Clauses.<\/p>\r\n

                      Incorrect Sentences: He was very hungry, he ate a whole bun.<\/p>\r\n

                      Correct sentence: He was very hungry, so he ate a whole bun.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                        \r\n \t
                      1. Into\/In to<\/strong><\/li>\r\n<\/ol>\r\n

                        Into- It is a preposition that indicates movement towards the inside of the place.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                          \r\n \t
                        • \u00a0I came into the room<\/li>\r\n \t
                        • He fell into the trap.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                          In to- It just the combination of the individual words \u201cIN\u201d and \u201cTO\u201d<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                            \r\n \t
                          • I dropped in to see you.<\/li>\r\n \t
                          • I came in to tell you something.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n
                              \r\n \t
                            1. Its\/It\u2019s<\/strong><\/li>\r\n<\/ol>\r\n

                              Its- is used to indicate possession.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                                \r\n \t
                              • The dog is chasing its tail.<\/li>\r\n \t
                              • Its tail was long.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                                It\u2019s- is a contraction of \u201cit is\u201d or \u201cit has\u201d.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                                  \r\n \t
                                • It\u2019s an apple<\/li>\r\n \t
                                • Every Rose has its thorn.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n
                                    \r\n \t
                                  1. Watch\/Look\/See<\/strong><\/li>\r\n<\/ol>\r\n

                                    Watch\/ See\/ Look are often confusable in meaning. These verbs are used in different situations. Differentiation between these verbs are explained below:<\/p>\r\n

                                    Watch: Look at something carefully, when something is moving.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                                      \r\n \t
                                    • Watch TV<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                                      Look: To look at something directly.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                                        \r\n \t
                                      • Look at that strange man<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                                        See: Notice or became aware of things.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                                          \r\n \t
                                        • See that bird.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n
                                            \r\n \t
                                          1. To\/Too<\/strong><\/li>\r\n<\/ol>\r\n

                                            To- is a preposition with several meanings, including towards and until. It shows the direction.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                                              \r\n \t
                                            • I gave my books to her.<\/li>\r\n \t
                                            • We walked to the shop.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                                              Too- It is an adverb that can also mean, as well as additionally.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                                                \r\n \t
                                              • I am too excited.<\/li>\r\n \t
                                              • You can sing too.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n
                                                  \r\n \t
                                                1. Lose\/loose<\/strong><\/li>\r\n<\/ol>\r\n

                                                  Lose: It is a verb that means, failed or didn\u2019t win at something or \u201cto suffer a loss\u201d or \u201cto miss\u201d<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                                                    \r\n \t
                                                  • The team will lose if they don\u2019t play well.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                                                    Loose: It is an adjective that means \u201cnot tight\u201d<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                                                      \r\n \t
                                                    • She is wearing a loose dress.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n
                                                        \r\n \t
                                                      1. Could\/Would\/Should<\/strong><\/li>\r\n<\/ol>\r\n

                                                        Could: It is the past forms of can and can\u2019t.It used to make polite requests, possibilities, and suggestions.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                                                          \r\n \t
                                                        • Could you please open that window?<\/li>\r\n \t
                                                        • We could have left the party earlier.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                                                          Would: Used to talk about unreal or unlikely situations, or imagining results.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                                                            \r\n \t
                                                          • You would be healthier if you exercise regularly.<\/li>\r\n \t
                                                          • If I were the CEO of my company, I would make a lot of changes.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                                                            Should: It used for advising or suggesting someone.<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                                                              \r\n \t
                                                            • You should go to the doctor. You look sick.<\/li>\r\n \t
                                                            • We should go home.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                                                              English Languages<\/a> has its own tricky rules, it only requires a little practice to become Master the Grammar.<\/p>\r\n\r\n
