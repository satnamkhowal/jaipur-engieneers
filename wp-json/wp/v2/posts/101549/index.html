    How To Calculate Percentage In Excel<\/strong><\/h2>\r\n

    Formula for percentage of total in Excel <\/strong><\/h3>\r\n

    Let’s now see the simplest way Excel teaches us to calculate percentages.<\/p>\r\n\r\n\r\n

    \r\n
    The basic formula to calculate the percentage of a total in Excel is<\/strong><\/h6>\r\n
    Percentage = (Part\/Total)<\/strong><\/h6>\r\n<\/div>\r\n\r\n

    To display this result as a percentage (e.g., 25% instead of 0.25), you’ll then need to format the cell containing this formula as a percentage.<\/p>\r\n

    Explanation<\/strong><\/p>\r\n

      \r\n
    • Part<\/strong>: This represents the specific value or quantity you want to express as a percentage of the whole. It’s the numerator in the fraction.<\/li>\r\n
    • Total<\/strong>: This represents the entire amount or sum that the “Part” belongs to. It’s the denominator in the fraction.<\/li>\r\n
    • \/<\/strong>: This is the division operator. Excel divides the “Part” by the “Total” to get a decimal value representing the proportion.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

      The Basic formula for percentage in Excel <\/strong><\/h3>\r\n

      Calculating a percentage involves dividing the part by the whole and multiplying by 100. In Excel, you can achieve this directly or by formatting the result as a percentage.<\/p>\r\n

      Method 1: Direct Calculation<\/strong><\/h3>\r\n
        \r\n
      • Identify the “Part” and the “Whole”: Determine which value represents the portion you’re interested in (the part) and which value represents the total (the whole).<\/li>\r\n
      • Enter the Data: Input your “part” and “whole” values into separate cells in your Excel sheet. For example, you might have the “part” in cell A2 and the “whole” in cell B2.<\/li>\r\n
      • Write the Excel percentage formula: <\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n\r\n\r\n
        \r\n
        In an empty cell where you want the percentage to appear, enter the following formula:<\/strong><\/h6>\r\n
        =(A2\/B2)*100<\/strong><\/h6>\r\n<\/div>\r\n\r\n
          \r\n
        • Replace A2 with the cell containing the “part” value.<\/li>\r\n
        • Replace B2 with the cell containing the “whole” value.<\/li>\r\n
        • The *100 at the end converts the decimal result into a percentage.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

          Press Enter: Excel will calculate the result.<\/p>\r\n

          Method 2: Formatting as a Percentage<\/strong><\/h3>\r\n

          This method performs the division and then formats the resulting decimal as a percentage, which is often cleaner.<\/p>\r\n

            \r\n
          • Identify the “Part” and the “Whole”:<\/strong> As in Method 1.<\/li>\r\n
          • Enter the Data:<\/strong> Input your “part” and “whole” values into separate cells.<\/li>\r\n
          • Write the Division Formula:<\/strong> In an empty cell, enter the formula to divide the part by the whole:<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n\r\n
            \r\n
            Excel = A2\/B2<\/strong><\/h6>\r\n\r\n<\/div>\r\n\r\n
              \r\n
            • Again, adjust A2 and B2 to match your data.<\/li>\r\n
            • Press Enter:<\/strong> Excel will display the result as a decimal (e.g., 0.25).<\/li>\r\n
            • Format as Percentage:<\/strong><\/li>\r\n\r\n
                \r\n
              • Select the cell containing the decimal result.<\/li>\r\n
              • Go to the Home tab on the Excel ribbon.<\/li>\r\n
              • In the Number group, click the Percent Style button (it looks like a % symbol).<\/li>\r\n
              • Alternatively, you can press Ctrl + Shift + % (or Cmd + Shift + % on a Mac).<\/li>\r\n
              • Excel will format the decimal as a percentage (e.g., 25%). You can further adjust the number of decimal places displayed using the Increase Decimal and Decrease Decimal buttons in the Number group.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul><\/ul>\r\n\r\n

                Examples<\/strong><\/h3>\r\n

                Let’s illustrate with three common scenarios:<\/p>\r\n

                Example 1: Calculating the Percentage of Sales Achieved<\/strong><\/p>\r\n

                  \r\n
                • Scenario:<\/strong> Your sales target for the month was 50,000, and you achieved 35,000 in sales. What percentage of your target did you reach?<\/li>\r\n
                • Data:<\/strong><\/strong><\/li>\r\n
                    \r\n
                  • Cell A2 (Part – Sales Achieved): 35000<\/li>\r\n
                  • Cell B2 (Whole – Sales Target): 50000<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                    Formula (in cell C2 using Method 1):<\/strong><\/p>\r\n\r\n\r\n

                    \r\n
                    Excel formula =(A2\/B2)*100<\/strong><\/h6>\r\n\r\n<\/div>\r\n\r\n\r\n\r\n
                      \r\n
                    • Result:<\/strong> 70<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                      Formula (in cell C2 using Method 2):<\/strong><\/p>\r\n\r\n\r\n\r\n

                      \r\n
                      Excel=A2\/B2<\/strong><\/h6>\r\n\r\n<\/div>\r\n\r\n\r\n

                      Example 2: Finding the Percentage Increase<\/strong><\/p>\r\n

                        \r\n
                      • Scenario:<\/strong> Last year, your company had 200 employees. This year, you have 230 employees. What is the percentage increase in the number of employees?<\/li>\r\n
                      • Data:<\/strong><\/strong><\/li>\r\n
                          \r\n
                        • Cell A2 (Original Value – Last Year’s Employees): 200<\/li>\r\n
                        • Cell B2 (New Value – This Year’s Employees): 230<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                          Formula (in cell C2):<\/strong> To find the percentage increase, you calculate the difference, divide by the original value, and then multiply by 100:<\/p>\r\n\r\n\r\n

                          \r\n
                          Excel = ((B2-A2)\/A2)*100<\/strong><\/h6>\r\n\r\n<\/div>\r\n\r\n\r\n

                          Result:<\/strong> 15<\/p>\r\n

                          Formula (in cell C2 using Method 2 for the division):<\/strong><\/strong><\/p>\r\n\r\n

                          \r\n
                          Excel = (B2-A2)\/A2<\/strong><\/h6>\r\n\r\n<\/div>\r\n\r\n\r\n\r\n

                          <\/strong>Result:<\/strong> 0.15 After formatting cell C2 as a percentage:<\/strong> 15.00%<\/p>\r\n

                          Example 3: Calculating the Percentage of a Category within a Total<\/strong><\/p>\r\n

                            \r\n
                          • Scenario:<\/strong> You have a list of expenses given in rupees: Rent (15,000), Salaries (60,000), and Marketing (25,000). What percentage of the total expenses is spent on Marketing?<\/li>\r\n
                          • Data:<\/strong><\/strong><\/li>\r\n
                              \r\n
                            • Cell A2 (Expense Category): Rent<\/li>\r\n
                            • Cell B2 (Amount): 15000<\/li>\r\n
                            • Cell A3 (Expense Category): Salaries<\/li>\r\n
                            • Cell B3 (Amount): 60000<\/li>\r\n
                            • Cell A4 (Expense Category): Marketing<\/li>\r\n
                            • Cell B4 (Amount – Part): 25000<\/li>\r\n
                            • Cell B5 (Total Expenses – Whole): =SUM(B2:B4) (This will calculate 100,000)<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                              Formula (in cell C4 to find the percentage for Marketing using Method 1):<\/strong><\/strong><\/p>\r\n\r\n

                              \r\n
                              Excel = (B4\/B5)*100<\/strong><\/h6>\r\n\r\n<\/div>\r\n\r\n\r\n\r\n\r\n

                              <\/strong>Result:<\/strong> 25<\/p>\r\n

                              Formula (in cell C4 using Method 2):<\/strong><\/strong><\/p>\r\n

                              \r\n
                              Excel = B4\/B5<\/strong><\/h6>\r\n\r\n<\/div>\r\n\r\n\r\n\r\n\r\n

                              Result:<\/strong> 0.25 After formatting cell C4 as a percentage:<\/strong> 25.00%.<\/p>\r\n
