What do you mean by Performance Marketing?<\/strong><\/h2>\r\n

Performance marketing is nothing but a digital strategy where advertisers pay only when predefined actions that are measurable (sales or leads) are completed by users. This results-oriented approach focuses on achieving specific business outcomes and optimises your return on investment. It manages various online channels with transparent tracking methods and accountability for the money spent on marketing.<\/p>\r\n

Performance Marketing Examples<\/strong><\/h3>\r\n
    \r\n
  • For example, consider you are an influencer with a handsome number of followers. A clothing brand partners with you by giving you unique coupon codes to share with your followers. The brand only pays you a commission for every sale made using their specific code. You can earn money only when the brand gets sales through your followers.

    <\/li>\r\n
  • A software company runs Facebook ads promoting a fun quiz related to their industry. They only pay to Facebook when a qualified lead is generated. This means when someone completes the quiz and provides their contact information, the company pays to Facebook. <\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n\r\n\r\n\r\n\r\n

    Types of Performance Marketing<\/strong><\/h2>\r\n
      \r\n
    • Cost Per Acquisition (CPA):<\/strong> Advertisers pay only when a specific action, a sale, lead form submission, or app install, is completed. <\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

      Example:<\/strong> A company spends Rs. 5000 on ads and gets 50 new customers. Their CPA is Rs. 100 (5000 \/ 50).<\/p>\r\n

        \r\n
      • Cost Per Click (CPC):<\/strong> Advertisers pay each time someone clicks on their ad, regardless of what happens after the click. <\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

        Example:<\/strong> A business runs a Google Ads campaign and pays an average of Rs.10 every time a user clicks their link.<\/p>\r\n

          \r\n
        • Cost Per Mille (CPM) or Cost Per Thousand Impressions:<\/strong> Ad makers shall pay a fixed amount for every 1,000 times their ad is displayed (impressions), regardless of clicks or actions. <\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

          Example:<\/strong> A brand pays Rs. 500 CPM for a display ad on a website, meaning they pay Rs. 500 for every 1,000 views of their ad.<\/p>\r\n\r\n\r\n

                        Digital Marketing Performance Metrics<\/strong><\/h2>\r\n

                        Digital marketing performance metrics are measurements that are used to track, analyze, and assess the success\/effectiveness of digital marketing campaigns and efforts. By carefully monitoring these metrics, marketers can see what’s working, find out the areas for improvement, and change their strategies to maximize their return on investment. They can be classified as:<\/p>\r\n\r\n

                          \r\n
                        • Website & Traffic metrics<\/li>\r\n
                        • Engagement metrics<\/li>\r\n
                        • Conversion metrics<\/li>\r\n
                        • Sales and revenue metrics<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                          Website & Traffic metrics<\/strong><\/h3>\r\n
                            \r\n
                          • Website Traffic:<\/strong> The total number of visitors to your website over a specific period. <\/li>\r\n
                          • Traffic Sources:<\/strong> How and where your visitors are coming from (e.g., organic search, social media, direct, referrals). This helps you to understand which channels drive traffic.<\/li>\r\n
                          • Bounce Rate: <\/strong>The visitors’ percentage who leave your website after seeing only one page. A high bounce rate indicates poor content quality. <\/li>\r\n
                          • Average Session Duration: <\/strong>The average time that visitors spend on your website. Longer durations mean high viewership.<\/li>\r\n
                          • Pages Per Session:<\/strong> The total number of pages a visitor viewed on an average during a single session. More pages viewed show a deeper interest.                             Engagement metrics<\/strong><\/h3>\r\n
                              \r\n
                            • Click-Through Rate (CTR):<\/strong> The percentage of how many people who clicked on a link (in an ad, email, or social media post) after seeing it. A high CTR suggests your message is relevant.<\/li>\r\n
                            • Engagement Rate (Social Media):<\/strong> Measures how users connect with your social media contents (likes, comments, shares).<\/li>\r\n
                            • Time on Page:<\/strong> Indicates how much time a user spends on every page. <\/li>\r\n
                            • Scroll Depth:<\/strong> How far down a webpage users scroll. Shows whether the user reads the entire content.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n

                              Conversion metrics<\/strong><\/h3>\r\n
                                \r\n
                              • Conversion Rate:<\/strong> The visitors’ percentage who have completed a desired action like purchase or sign-up. You can directly measure how well your marketing has turned interest into results.<\/li>\r\n
                              • Cost Per Conversion (CPC):<\/strong> The average cost you pay for each successful conversion. CPC is important to assess the efficiency of paid campaigns.<\/li>\r\n
                              • Lead Generation Rate:<\/strong> The website visitors’ percentage or ad clicks that result in a lead (e.g., filling out a contact form).<\/li>\r\n
                              • Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):<\/strong> The total cost required to fetch a new customer through marketing efforts.<\/li>\r\n<\/ul>\r\n\r\n
